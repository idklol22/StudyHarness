Themes: midnight, smoke, graphite, contrast, red, orange, ember, emerald, ocean, sapphire, citrus, blue (light),

## Principles (the owner was explicit: no one-size-fits-all copy)
1. **Semantic tokens, not raw colours.** Every component uses only role tokens: `--bg`, `--surface`, `--surface-2`,
   `--surface-raised`, `--border`, `--border-strong`, `--text`, `--text-muted`, `--text-faint`, `--accent`,
   `--accent-contrast` (text on accent), `--focus`, `--danger`, `--success`, `--warning`, `--selection`,
   `--scrim` (behind text over a scene), `--shadow`. Each theme defines all of them. No component hard-codes a colour.
2. **Per-theme tuning, not just a hue swap.** Light themes need darker borders, real shadows, a darker accent for
   contrast, and inverted image treatments. Saturated themes (red, orange, ember, citrus) need a neutralised
   `--text-muted` (not tinted mush) and an accent that isn't the same hue as the danger colour (red theme: danger
   shifts toward magenta/white with an icon so it's still distinct). Contrast theme: pure black/white, 2px focus,
   no glass, no scenes.
3. **Readability is tested.** A pure `contrastRatio(a,b)` and a test that, for every theme, checks: text/bg ≥ 7,
   text/surface ≥ 7, text-muted on bg and surface ≥ 4.5, text-faint ≥ 3, accent-contrast/accent ≥ 4.5, border-strong
   vs surface ≥ 3, focus vs bg ≥ 3. Category/subject colours: each app maps its palette per theme (lightness adjusted
   so the swatch is ≥ 3:1 against the surface, and the label text drawn in it is ≥ 4.5:1). Fix the tokens until all
   pass. Never lower the thresholds.
4. **Scenes and glass.** Scene themes (midnight particles, nimbus clouds, uzumaki swirl, smoke) render the scene on a
   fixed canvas behind the app at low cost: one canvas, paused when the tab is hidden, reduced or static with
   `prefers-reduced-motion`, and off on battery-saver/low-end devices (a setting "Background animation: on/off").
   Glass tiles: `backdrop-filter: blur()` surfaces with a `--scrim` so text contrast holds over the brightest part of
   the scene, with a solid fallback when backdrop-filter is unsupported. Long-reading surfaces (PDF reader, notes
   editor, chat messages) always sit on an opaque surface, never on glass.
5. **Each app keeps its own identity.** Apply the themes through each app's existing structure (the Learn Harness:
   shadcn/Tailwind tokens in `app/globals.css` + `src/core/themes.ts`; the Planner: `src/theme.css`) by mapping the
   semantic tokens onto that app's variables. Make each app's special parts look intentional in every theme:
   - Learn Harness: the 3D knowledge graph and 2D map read their colours from tokens (re-read on theme change); PDF
     pages stay white paper with a subtle shadow in every theme; code blocks get a matching syntax palette for light
     vs dark; the chat composer, side panel and sidebar must look right.
   - Planner: the flower checkboxes/blobs, the category cards, the calories bar and the calendar weekend colours (Sat
     and Sun colours per theme, readable).
6. **Theme picker.** A grid of theme cards (a small live preview of bg/surface/accent/text plus the scene thumbnail),
   in Settings. Stored per device. It applies instantly with no flash on load (inline script in the head sets
   `data-theme` before paint). Defaults: the Learn Harness keeps its current black/white as a theme named "mono"
   (default); the Planner defaults to midnight.

   nimbus (scene) and uzumaki. Look at them and fix anything hard to read before finishing.
